Dataset Preprocessing and Cleaning

This commit outlines the detailed steps, logic, and sampling criteria applied during the preprocessing of the medical symptom datasets to prepare them for the LLM fine-tuning phase (Qwen 3.5 2B model).

 1. Datasets Used
- hf-gretelAI (Primary Source): `train.jsonl`, `test.jsonl`
  - A classification dataset mapping raw patient symptom descriptions to 22 disease labels.
- k-prognosis (Supplementary Source): `Training.csv`, `Testing.csv`
  - A binary matrix classification dataset mapping 132 sparse symptom indicators to 41 disease labels. Used to extract extra symptom descriptions for endemic diseases missing from the primary source.

 2. Target Objectives
- Output Format: ChatML format compatible with Instruct/Chat LLMs (`system`, `user`, `assistant` conversational turns).
- Dataset Size: Exactly 1,200 ChatML pairs.
- Class Coverage: Full coverage across 24 distinct disease classes, ensuring exactly 50 descriptions per class (Balanced dataset).
- Constraints: Assistant responses must be strictly under 120 words to keep the model concise and focused.

 3. Cleaning & Preprocessing Steps

 A. Input Text Standardization (The Patient Query)
The raw symptom descriptions ("input_text") underwent several normalization sweeps to eliminate noise and improve model learning efficiency:
1. Artifact Removal: Stripped out any synthetic formatting tokens generated by previous LLMs (e.g., `<|endoftext|>`, `[USER]`, `[BOT]`, `[INST]`).
2. Whitespace Normalization: Removed leading/trailing whitespace, and collapsed multiple consecutive spaces into a single space.
3. Punctuation Correction: Fixed hanging punctuation and typos, such as multiple periods (`..` $\rightarrow$ `.`) and spaces before punctuation (` ,` $\rightarrow$ `,`).
4. Formatting: Ensured every symptom description query started with a capitalized letter.

 B. Label Normalization & Enrichment
The 22 raw classes in `hf-gretelAI` were mapped to clean, clinically accurate display names (e.g., "gastroesophageal reflux disease" $\rightarrow$ "GERD"). 

To reach the requested 24 disease classes and better represent rural Indian endemic healthcare profiles, two major diseases were injected into the dataset by synthesizing and pulling natural language descriptions corresponding to their symptoms:
1. Tuberculosis (Endemic Respiratory)
2. Gastroenteritis (Endemic Gastrointestinal)

 C. Conversational Turn Construction
For each row, the data was formatted into a 3-turn ChatML sequence:

- Turn 1 (System Prompt):
  > "You are ArogyaMitra, an empathetic rural healthcare AI assistant. Provide concise differential diagnostic logic, reflect symptoms clearly, explain potential causes simply, and outline self-monitoring steps and warning flags under 120 words."

- Turn 2 (User):
  > The cleaned patient symptom query.

- Turn 3 (Assistant):
  > The assistant's generated response was heavily standardized to fulfill the clinical requirements without exceeding the 120-word limit. The response framework structurally includes:
  > 1. Reflection & Primary Suspect: Validating the patient's symptoms and naming the most likely condition.
  > 2. Differential Possibilities: Naming 1-2 alternate closely related medical conditions.
  > 3. Self-Monitoring Steps: Actionable, at-home care instructions.
  > 4. 🚨 Red Flags: Immediate emergency warning signs that warrant an urgent clinical visit.

 4. Sampling Criteria & Balancing Strategy
To ensure the LLM isn't biased towards any specific condition, the dataset was strictly balanced:

1. Mapping and Aggregation: Grouped all cleaned user symptom descriptions by their respective disease label.
2. Upsampling/Downsampling:
   - If a disease class had more than 50 descriptions, we randomly sampled exactly 50 descriptions.
   - If a disease class had fewer than 50 descriptions (e.g., Jaundice had 40), we sampled all available unique descriptions and then randomly re-sampled (with replacement) the remaining difference to reach 50.
3. Endemic Injections: Exactly 50 robust symptom scenarios were generated and assigned to both Tuberculosis and Gastroenteritis.

 5. Output Results
- Total Processed Rows: 1,200 ChatML pairs.
- Total Disease Classes: 24 classes.
- Distribution: 50 pairs per disease class (perfectly balanced).
- Destination: The final artifact `hf-gretel-clean.json` was generated and exported safely to the `datasets/fine-tuning/` directory.

 6. Repository Integrity (`.gitignore`)
To prevent data leaks and keep the remote repository light, both the clean output and the dirty raw datasets have been isolated. The `.gitignore` at the repository root and backend directory was successfully appended with:
```gitignore
 Ignore fine-tuning datasets (both clean and unclean)
datasets/fine-tuning/
```
